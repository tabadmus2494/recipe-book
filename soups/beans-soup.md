# African Beans Soup `gbegiri`

## Ingredients

- 1 cup ground beans flour (brown beans or black-eyed beans variety)
- 3–4 cups water (adjust to your preferred thickness)
- 3 tablespoons (tbsp) palm oil
- 1 medium onion (blended or finely chopped)
- 2 medium tomatoes (blended; optional, depending on taste)
- 1–2 scotch bonnet peppers (ata rodo) or any hot chilli (blended with tomato and onion)
- 0.5 tbsp ground crayfish (optional but adds authentic flavour)
- 1–2 seasoning cubes (e.g. Maggi or Knorr)
- Salt to taste
- A pinch of ground locust beans (iru/ogiri) – optional but very traditional

## Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the bean base
- Mix the ground beans flour with a little water into a smooth paste (no lumps).
- Add more water (about 3–4 cups) and stir well.
- Pour into a pot and cook on medium heat, stirring constantly to prevent lumps. It will thicken as it cooks, like a porridge.
  
### Step 2: Cook until soft
- Allow the beans mixture to cook for about 10–15 minutes, stirring often. It should look smooth and slightly thick.

### Step 3: Make the palm oil sauce
- In a separate small pan, heat the palm oil gently (don't bleach it).
- Add the blended onion, tomato, and pepper mix. Fry for 5–7 minutes until the raw taste disappears.
- Add crayfish, seasoning cubes, locust beans (if using), and a little salt. Stir well.

### Step 4: Combine the sauce with the beans
- Pour the fried palm oil mixture into the cooked bean base.
- Stir thoroughly and let it simmer together for another 5–10 minutes on low heat.
- Taste and adjust seasoning.

### Step 5: Serve
- Your Nigerian bean soup (gbegiri) is ready.
- It's best enjoyed with amala, eba, fufu, or even white rice.

`Note`:
- If you want it richer, you can add smoked fish, goat meat stock, or assorted meats.
- Some people skip tomatoes and just use onion + pepper + palm oil for a more traditional flavour.
- Adjust the thickness by adding more or less water.
